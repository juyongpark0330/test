How it is calculated
  • Takt time = available production time ÷ demand
  • Required operators = ⌈ work content ÷ takt time ⌉
  • Capacity = available time ÷ (work content ÷ operators on the line)
  • Utilisation = work content ÷ (required operators × takt time)

Crews are counted in whole people, so the requirement is rounded up. Any gap between the work content and the crew's takt capacity shows up as idle time inside the cycle — that is why utilisation normally sits below 100%.

How it is calculated
  • Availability = run time ÷ planned production time
  • Performance = (ideal cycle time × total count) ÷ run time
  • Quality = good count ÷ total count
  • OEE = availability × performance × quality

The three losses are converted back into minutes so they can be compared on one bar: availability loss is the recorded downtime, performance loss is the time lost to running slower than the ideal cycle, and quality loss is the ideal time tied up in rejects. A figure above 85% is world class; 60% is typical for discrete manufacturing.

How it is calculated
  • Bottleneck = the largest cycle time on the line
  • LOB = total cycle time ÷ (stations × bottleneck cycle time)
  • Idle time per cycle = stations × bottleneck − total cycle time
  • Output = 3,600 ÷ bottleneck cycle time

Aim for 85–95%. Moving or splitting work out of the bottleneck raises output without buying equipment — the line only ever moves as fast as its slowest station.

How it is calculated
  • UPH = 3,600 ÷ effective cycle time
  • Effective cycle = cycle time ÷ efficiency, or work content ÷ (operators × efficiency)
  • Shift output = UPH × (shift minutes − planned stops) ÷ 60

Efficiency carries everything that keeps a real line from running at its rated pace: allowances, minor stops, waiting for material and operator pace. It is not a target to be optimistic about — take it from your own line's recent days.
